# Final Diagnosis and Historical Identification Audit

## Verdict in one paragraph

The structural work establishes a conditional result: if humanity's relevant
global condition includes death, and if restoration must occur by
representative participation rather than sovereign authority alone, then the
representative must enter death and emerge beyond it. Christianity uniquely
among the compared source lanes claims that mechanism. History strongly
supports Jesus' existence and execution and very early resurrection belief;
history also supports that named followers reported experiences interpreted as
appearances. Historical method does not independently establish the bodily
resurrection, divine identity, sinlessness, universal authority, or global
atoning role. Those remain inference, theological identification, or open
adjudication rather than historical givens.

## Test 1 — Is the human condition globally death-bound?

| Claim | Status | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Human biological mortality is universal | Empirically supported | Does not by itself connect death to guilt or sin |
| Harm propagates relationally and across generations | Empirically supported in ordinary experience | Does not imply a transferable metaphysical debt |
| Every person is both victim and culpable offender | Theological/anthropological claim | Too strong as a bare empirical universal; culpability depends on agency, knowledge, and freedom |
| Sin and death form one global reign | Christian theological diagnosis | Explicit in Romans 5; not independently delivered by biology |
| Bodily death, corruption, alienation, and moral fracture are one integrated condition | Bridge claim | Coherent within the framework but requires argument across categories |

### Test 1 result

The biological death-bound component passes. The full Christian diagnosis is
not assumption-free: the connection among death, sin, corruption, and
alienation is a disclosed theological bridge.

## Test 2 — Does Jesus instantiate the candidate profile?

| Candidate property | Status | Evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Belongs to humanity / existed historically | Historically strong | Multiple early Christian sources; non-Christian reception confirms the movement's executed founder |
| Executed under Pontius Pilate | Historically strong | Pauline tradition, Gospel traditions, and Tacitus Annals 15.44 |
| Death interpreted as “for sins” | Very early attestation | 1 Corinthians 15 preserves received tradition; attestation establishes early belief, not the mechanism's truth |
| Followers reported resurrection appearances | Historically strong as reported belief/experience | 1 Corinthians 15 names Cephas, the Twelve, James, others, and Paul |
| Empty tomb | Historically disputed | Defended by many scholars and rejected or reinterpreted by others |
| Bodily resurrection occurred | Contested historical/metaphysical inference | Not equivalent to establishing early belief or appearance claims |
| Voluntarily came in order to die | Textually claimed; historical self-understanding debated | Requires evaluation of Gospel sayings and their transmission |
| Free of culpability/sin | Theological identification | Not established by ordinary historical method |
| Divinely authorized | Theological identification | Not established merely by execution or resurrection belief |
| Represents the whole human population | Pauline/theological identification | Romans 5 and 1 Corinthians 15 supply the claim; history alone does not |
| Restorative capacity is genuinely global | Open unless resurrection and representative claims hold | This is close to the target conclusion and must not be used as an input |

## Primary and historical anchors

- 1 Corinthians 15:3-28 — received death, burial, resurrection, and appearance
  tradition; Christ as firstfruits and death as final enemy.
- Romans 5:12-21 — population-wide Adam/Christ structure connecting sin, death,
  grace, and life.
- Romans 6:3-11 — union with Christ's death and resurrection.
- Hebrews 2:14-18 — shared humanity and action through death.
- Tacitus, Annals 15.44 — hostile Roman witness to Christus' execution under
  Pontius Pilate and the subsequent Christian movement.
- Quran 4:157-158 — a live rival account rejecting Jesus' crucifixion and
  affirming divine raising, demonstrating that sovereign-authority restoration
  does not require representative death within that framework.

## Final calibrated conclusion

### Established structurally

- Generic global restoration does not entail a death.
- Authority over death can be modeled without the restorer dying.
- Representative same-condition participation in a death-bound population
  entails that the representative enters death.
- If the representative carries the population through the condition, emergence
  beyond death is required for successful completion.

### Established historically at high confidence

- Jesus was executed under Pontius Pilate.
- Resurrection belief and claimed appearances arose very early in the Christian
  movement.

### Not established by Lean or ordinary history alone

- That Jesus bodily rose.
- That Jesus is divine, sinless, universally authorized, or humanity's unique
  representative.
- That representative participation is metaphysically necessary rather than
  sovereign authority alone.

### Strongest honest synthesis

Christianity uniquely supplies the tested representative death-and-resurrection
mechanism, and Jesus' death plus the earliest resurrection proclamation have a
real historical anchor. The complete conclusion remains conditional on the
truth of the resurrection and the theological identifications. The experiment
has not reduced Christianity to a tautology, but neither has it converted its
central historical-theological claims into assumption-free Lean theorems.

## Resurrection rival-hypothesis comparison

The fixed evidence lane includes execution/death, very early resurrection
proclamation, named appearance tradition, diversity of claimed appearances,
rapid movement formation, and a disputed empty-tomb lane.

- Resurrection covers the full selected lane if divine action is admitted.
- Visions alone do not explain an empty tomb.
- Body relocation alone does not explain appearances or resurrection belief.
- Slow legend alone fits poorly with very early proclamation.
- Survival conflicts with the fixed death datum.
- A composite relocation-plus-visions hypothesis can be assigned full coverage
  without divine action, but requires multiple independently coordinated events.

Coverage therefore does not uniquely select resurrection. The comparison turns
on source-level fit, independent motivation, complexity, and whether divine
action has a non-negligible prior probability. Lean does not assign those.
