# Religious Comparison Evidence Ledger

## Boundary

The Lean file records whether a blinded profile claims to cover a category. It
does not establish the truth, historical occurrence, or uniqueness of any
religion. `supported` means supported as a claim within the selected source
lane. `disputed` preserves substantial internal variation.

## Unblinding key

| Lean profile | Source lane |
|---|---|
| TraditionA | Christianity, Pauline death-resurrection lane |
| TraditionB | Judaism, Ezekiel 18 repentance/responsibility lane |
| TraditionC | Islam, Quranic responsibility/mercy/resurrection lane |
| TraditionD | Early Buddhism, Four Noble Truths lane |
| TraditionE | Hindu traditions, Bhagavad Gita surrender/liberation lane |

## Primary-source anchors

### TraditionA — Christianity

- Romans 5:12-21: sin and death have population-wide scope; grace and life are
  associated with Jesus Christ.
- 1 Corinthians 15:3-28: Christ dies for sins, is raised, is called firstfruits,
  and death is presented as the final enemy.

### TraditionB — Judaism

- Ezekiel 18: individual responsibility, repentance, and turning from
  transgression. Later Jewish positions on resurrection and final judgment are
  internally diverse, so those fields remain `disputed` here.

### TraditionC — Islam

- Quran 6:164: no bearer bears another person's burden.
- Quran 39:53: divine mercy and forgiveness of sins.
- Resurrection and judgment are central Quranic claims, while representative
  burden-bearing and the Christian death-resurrection mechanism are rejected.

### TraditionD — Early Buddhism

- SN 56.11: suffering, craving, cessation, and the path. Moral debt to a divine
  lawgiver and bodily resurrection are not the selected lane's diagnosis or
  mechanism.

### TraditionE — Bhagavad Gita lane

- Bhagavad Gita 18:66: refuge in Krishna and promised liberation from sins.
  Hindu schools interpret agency, grace, karma, selfhood, and liberation
  differently; disputed and partial fields must not be flattened.

## Current result boundary

- A, B, and C claim the selected common moral-relational coverage.
- A and C claim the selected judgment-and-bodily-restoration coverage through
  different mechanisms.
- Only A claims representative burden-bearing through a restorer who enters
  and overcomes death.
- This distinguishes mechanisms but does not prove that A's distinctive
  mechanism is necessary or historically true.

## Participation versus authority hinge

- Hebrews 2:14-18 presents a participation mechanism: the restorer shares
  flesh and blood and acts through death.
- Romans 6:3-11 presents union with the restorer's death and resurrection.
- Quran 4:157-158 rejects Jesus' crucifixion and instead says God raised him;
  this supplies a live authority-without-representative-death alternative.
- The common observable target cannot by itself decide between sovereign
  authority and representative participation. The need for death follows only
  if representative continuity is independently required.
